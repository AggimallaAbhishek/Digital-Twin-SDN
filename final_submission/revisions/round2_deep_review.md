## Summary
This paper presents a closed-loop software-defined networking (SDN) control architecture for campus Wi-Fi (emulated via Mininet-WiFi and Ryu) that gates both heuristic optimization actions and schema-validated Large Language Model (LLM) intent translations behind an analytical digital twin verifier, paired with post-execution KPI monitoring and automatic rollback. Evaluated across 27 runs spanning three disruption scenarios (lecture flash crowd, access-point failure, and co-channel interference), the study investigates whether pre-verifying candidate actions via fluid airtime sharing, distance-weighted interference, and calibrated M/M/1/K queueing improves recovery speed and operational safety. Most notably, the authors transparently document important negative and inconclusive empirical results: although the analytical twin achieves a 4.5% steady-state throughput error in open-loop testing and executes in a median 0.61 ms, its closed-loop predictions during live network transients perform no better than a "nothing changes" baseline (missing 21 of 23 harmful actions), leaving safety protection to the post-execution watch-and-rollback step, which itself fails when rolling clients back onto a newly downed access point.

## Strengths
* **Exemplary Scientific Transparency and Empirical Rigor on Negative Results:** Rather than overclaiming the capabilities of AI-driven and digital-twin-gated automation, the authors rigorously diagnose and report where steady-state analytical twins break down during live closed-loop wireless transients (missing 21 of 23 harmful actions) and how automated rollbacks can fail during concurrent infrastructure faults, offering highly valuable cautionary insights backed by a fully reproducible artifact package.
* **Clean Privilege Separation in the LLM Intent Pipeline:** The architecture enforces strict safety boundaries by isolating the LLM to natural-language-to-JSON policy parsing under schema validation with zero direct tool access to mutate the network, relying on a deterministic compiler and an append-only executor ledger to ensure no syntactically invalid or out-of-bounds configurations reach the data plane.
* **Fast, Interpretable Analytical Verification:** By combining fluid max-min airtime sharing with a calibrated M/M/1/K queueing surrogate rebuilt from a sliding 10-second telemetry window without accumulated state drift, the twin achieves sub-millisecond evaluation speeds (median 0.61 ms) suitable for a 5-second control loop while accurately predicting held-out steady-state per-flow throughput within 4.5%.

## Weaknesses
* **[W1] Confounding Between the Digital Twin Gate and Operator Availability in V2 vs. V3:** In the unattended evaluation campaign, comparing V2 ("no twin") against V3 ("full") varies both twin verification and human-in-the-loop gating simultaneously, as V3 holds high-impact channel changes in an approval queue for an absent operator while V2 applies them immediately. Please consider adding an auto-approving V3 ablation (with matched approval delay) or enforcing the operator gate in V2 so the causal filtering effect of the digital twin is cleanly isolated across all disruption scenarios.
* **[W2] Circular Co-Channel Interference Emulation and Simplified Analytical Surrogates:** Because Mininet-WiFi does not natively emulate co-channel contention, the testbed artificially enforces the exact same load-independent, distance-based rate-cap equation (Eq. 1) used by the twin, and the M/M/1/K latency surrogate (Eq. 3) caps predicted delay at 15.8 ms while ignoring re-association transients and non-Markovian traffic bursts. The authors might consider validating the twin against physical-layer contention tracking or comparing against learned queueing surrogates on the recorded calibration dataset to bridge the reality gap in closed-loop predictions (Reference Paper: Advancing Next Generation Wireless Networks With Digital Twin: Construction, Validation, and Real-World Applications on an Indoor Over-the-Air Testbed, Berk Akgun et al., 2024; Reference Paper: RouteNet-Fermi: Network Modeling With Graph Neural Networks, Miquel Ferriol-Galmés et al., 2023; Reference Paper: Network Digital Twin Toward Networking, Telecommunications, and Traffic Engineering: A Survey, Reza Poorzare and Oliver P. Waldhorst, 2025).
* **[W3] Underpowered Statistical Design, Seed Reuse, and Degenerate V1 Recovery Baseline:** Relying on only $n = 3$ seeds that reuse the exact seed numbers (42, 43, 44) of the calibration dataset, applying symmetric Student-$t$ intervals to right-censored recovery times and bimodal rollback failures, and pooling sequentially dependent actions in Fisher's exact test limits statistical validity, while the absence of load-driven client roaming in V1 leaves Hypothesis H1 inconclusive across all scenarios. Please consider evaluating on a larger, independent set of random seeds, reporting non-parametric metrics, and adding a baseline with standard client-side roaming to establish finite baseline recovery times (Reference Paper: IEEE Standard for Information Technology—Telecommunications and Information Exchange between Systems - Local and Metropolitan Area Networks—Specific Requirements - Part 11: Wireless LAN Medium Access Control (MAC) and Physical Layer (PHY) Specifications, IEEE LAN/MAN Standards Committee, 2021).
* **[W4] Incremental Architectural Novelty and Limited LLM Benchmark Scope:** Gating closed-loop network reconfigurations behind a pre-deployment digital twin and post-execution assurance monitoring builds incrementally upon established intent-based networking and LLM translation frameworks, and Hypothesis H3 is evaluated only outside the automated loop on a small, custom 30-intent dataset where the primary cloud model saturates at 30/30 accuracy. The authors might consider expanding the LLM evaluation to standardized configuration synthesis benchmarks and clarifying the execution fate of LLM-compiled policies under adversarial or ambiguous intents (Reference Paper: Network Digital Twin: Context, Enabling Technologies, and Opportunities, P. Almasan et al., 2022; Reference Paper: Intent-Based Networking – Concepts and Definitions, A. Clemm et al., 2022; Reference Paper: End-to-End Network SLA Quality Assurance for C-RAN: A Closed-Loop Management Method Based on Digital Twin Network, X.-S. Qiu et al., 2023; Reference Paper: Intent-Based Management of Next-Generation Networks: An LLM-Centric Approach, Abdelkader Mekrache et al., 2024; Reference Paper: NetConfEval: Can LLMs Facilitate Network Configuration?, Changjie Wang et al., 2024; Reference Paper: Net2d-LLM: Translating Structured Network Intents into CLI using LLMs with Execution in a Network Digital Twin, Jerônimo Menezes et al., 2025).

## Potential Issues And Suggestions
### [Introduction, Hypotheses & Related Work] (Pages: 1-2)
#### Potential Mistakes and Improvements

1. **[I1.1] Falsifiability and Censoring Logic of Hypothesis H1 (Abstract & Section I, Page 1):**
   - **Potential Concern:** In Section I, Hypothesis H1 is formulated as: *"in each of three disruption scenarios (congestion, AP failure, interference) the full system (V3) recovers at least 30% faster than plain SDN (V1), a run that never recovers being counted at its end."* Including the explicit operational rule that non-recovering runs are assigned the run-end time ($T_{\text{end}}$) implies that H1 is deterministically decidable on finite-horizon runs. However, in the Abstract and Section VII-A, the authors conclude that H1 is *"inconclusive, because plain SDN never recovered"* and treat the censored V1 recovery times as lower bounds that prevent evaluating the 30% reduction threshold. This creates two methodological tensions in how H1 is formulated and falsified:
     - First, because V1 neither steers clients nor changes channels (and stations do not perform load-driven roaming on their own, as noted in Section II), V1 is structurally incapable of recovering in the congestion and interference scenarios within any finite window. Consequently, any hypothesis requiring a percentage reduction relative to V1's uncensored recovery time cannot be resolved unless the capped metric at $T_{\text{end}}$ is accepted as the primary test statistic or V1 is paired with a baseline capable of recovery.
     - Second, H1 requires V3 to recover at least 30% faster in *all three* scenarios, yet in the co-channel interference scenario V3 itself never recovered in any of its three runs ($\ge 420 \pm 0\text{ s}$, identical to V1) because the required channel change is a high-impact action held for a human operator who is absent in the unattended campaign. Even if V1 were run for an arbitrarily long horizon, V3 achieved $0/3$ recoveries within the 600 s window in that scenario, which constitutes direct evidence *against* H1 in the interference scenario rather than an inconclusive result caused by V1's right-censoring.
   - **Suggestion:** Revise the formulation of H1 in Section I so that its decision rule is unambiguous and falsifiable under right-censoring (for example, defining H1 strictly on the capped recovery metric within the 600 s SLA window or on recovery success rate, and distinguishing between scenarios where V3 failed to recover versus scenarios where V3 recovered but V1 was right-censored).

2. **[I1.2] Weak Existential Formulation and Statistical Power of Hypothesis H2 (Section I, Page 1):**
   - **Potential Concern:** Section I defines H2 in a *"weak, existential form"*—testing only whether the digital twin blocks *any* ($\ge 1$) harmful action that V2 applies directly, while noting that missed harmful actions are also reported. Although the Abstract and Section VII-B transparently report that the twin missed 21 of 23 harmful actions in V2, had higher median relative error than a trivial *"nothing changes"* baseline (56.7% vs. 46.5% for throughput), and showed no statistically significant difference in harm rate between twin-rejected and twin-accepted actions ($2/3$ vs. $21/58$, $p = 0.32$), defining H2 existentially allows a formal safety hypothesis to be declared satisfied in Section VII-B despite the twin failing as a predictive filter. In a noisy environment, even an uninformative or random rejection rule that blocks a small fraction of proposals will almost certainly block at least one action that would have been followed by a KPI regression.
   - **Suggestion:** Consider stating H2 in Section I as a falsifiable discriminative or comparative hypothesis (e.g., requiring the twin to reject a specified majority of harmful actions or achieve a statistically higher rejection rate on harmful actions than on benign ones), and reporting that H2 is *not met* (while noting that 2 of 23 harmful actions were blocked). This would align the formal hypothesis test directly with the paper's core empirical finding regarding the closed-loop twin.

3. **[I1.3] Conflation of Stochastic LLM Translation and Deterministic Software Guards in Hypothesis H3 (Section I & Section II, Pages 1–2):**
   - **Potential Concern:** In Section I, H3 combines two distinct claims: (1) that an LLM with schema-constrained output translates at least 20 of 30 natural-language intents correctly, and (2) that *"no invalid configuration reaches the network."* As described in Contribution (iv) and Section II (*LLM-based intent networking*), the LLM has no tool to modify the network; its output must pass a JSON schema, is compiled into bounded actions by deterministic code, and is gated by the verifier. Consequently, the second clause of H3 is an architectural invariant enforced by deterministic software validation rather than an empirical property of the LLM. Additionally, while Section II cites NetConfEval [9] as a benchmark for LLM configuration synthesis, it does not explain why H3 is evaluated on a custom 30-intent set rather than adapted tasks from [9].
   - **Suggestion:** Separate the empirical evaluation of LLM translation accuracy from the deterministic guarantee of the schema-and-compiler gate in the statement of H3, and briefly clarify in Section II how the custom 30-intent set differs in scope (e.g., targeting wireless campus QoS and client-steering primitives) from wired configuration benchmarks such as NetConfEval [9].

4. **[I1.4] Apparent Contradiction Regarding Autonomous Client Roaming vs. Re-association in V1 (Section II, Page 2):**
   - **Potential Concern:** In Section II (*Programmable WLANs*, page 2), the text states: *"in our emulation stations never roam on their own, so V1 leaves them on the overloaded AP."* This unqualified statement appears to conflict with Table I and Section VII-A (page 4), which state for V1 that *"stations re-associate by themselves"* and that in the AP failure scenario *"the stations of ap2 re-associate by themselves, so V1 already works (4.8% loss)."*
   - **Suggestion:** Clarify in Section II that emulated stations in V1 do not perform *load-driven* roaming away from an active, overloaded AP (which requires IEEE 802.11k/v/r or controller-initiated steering), even though they automatically scan and re-associate when their current AP interface goes completely down.

### [System Architecture, Analytical Twin Formulation & Control Loop Methodology] (Pages: 2-4)
#### Potential Mistakes and Improvements

1. **[I2.1] Baseline Reference Ambiguity, Disjunctive Acceptance Bypassing, and Latency Ceiling Mismatch in Verifier Rule (S2) (Section III-B and Section IV, Eqs. 3–6):**
   - **Potential Concern:** There are three closely related mathematical and structural issues in how predicted KPIs are formulated and compared in the verifier gate:
     1. *Baseline Definition in $\delta_k$ and Division by Zero:* Section III-B (page 2) defines $\mathbf{k} = (T, L_{95}, \bar{\ell}, J)$ as the *measured* KPI vector of a set of flows, whereas Section IV-A (page 3) defines $\hat{\mathbf{k}} = f(s, a)$ as the simulator's *predicted* KPIs. Section IV-B (page 3) defines $\delta_k$ in Eq. (6) as the signed percentage improvement of KPI $k$ "between $\mathbf{k}(s_t)$ and $\hat{\mathbf{k}} = f(s_t, a_t)$." It is ambiguous whether $\mathbf{k}(s_t)$ denotes the live telemetry measurement stored in state $s_t$ or the digital twin's no-action steady-state baseline simulation $f(s_t, \emptyset)$. If $\mathbf{k}(s_t)$ is taken directly from live telemetry, then $\delta_k$ conflates steady-state modeling error and transient dynamics with the predicted action effect. Conversely, if $\delta_k$ compares $f(s_t, \emptyset)$ and $f(s_t, a_t)$, the notation $\mathbf{k}(s_t)$ conflates measured telemetry with simulated predictions. Additionally, the exact mathematical formula for $\delta_k$ is not written out, leaving unspecified how zero or near-zero baseline loss $\bar{\ell}(s_t)$ or zero throughput $T(s_t)$ (during an AP failure) is handled to avoid division by zero, or whether packet loss (already expressed in percent) is compared via relative percentage change or absolute percentage-point difference in Eq. (6) and (S4).
     2. *Scale Mismatch and Queue-Level Formulation of the Latency Surrogate:* In Eq. (3), the queue delay surrogate $d_q = d_0 + s L(\rho_q, K)$ scales the dimensionless steady-state mean number of packets in an M/M/1/K system $L(\rho_q, K)$ by a fixed constant $s = 2.5\text{ ms}$ rather than inversely with the effective service capacity $C_i$ (as required by Little's Law when co-channel interference reduces $C_i$ in Eq. 1). Because $\lim_{\rho_q \to \infty} L(\rho_q, K) = K$, $d_q$ is strictly bounded above by $d_0 + sK = 15.8\text{ ms}$ for any flow on an active AP, and because Eq. (3) assigns an identical deterministic mean delay $d_q$ to all flows sharing queue $q$ on an AP, taking the "nearest-rank 95th percentile of the predicted latencies of the video flows" captures only inter-AP or inter-queue variation rather than the 95th-percentile tail of a packet delay distribution. Consequently, the twin can never predict a violation of the $L_{95}(\tau) \le 50\text{ ms}$ recovery threshold in Eq. (5), and if $\delta_{L_{95}}$ compares $\hat{L}_{95} \le 15.8\text{ ms}$ against live congested measurements exceeding $16.7\text{ ms}$, any candidate action that keeps the AP up will automatically yield a predicted latency improvement $\delta_{L_{95}} > 5\%$.
     3. *Disjunctive Acceptance Bypassing via Client-Count Fairness:* Because Eq. (6) joins the non-regression clause ($\forall k: \delta_k \ge -5$) and the improvement clause ($\exists k: \delta_k > 5$) with a logical OR ($\lor$), any action predicted to improve even a single KPI by $>5\%$ is accepted unconditionally, even if the twin simultaneously predicts severe regressions ($\delta_k \ll -5\%$) in throughput, loss, or latency. Moreover, while $\delta_k$ is defined "over the flows the action affects," the fourth component of $\mathbf{k}$ is Jain's fairness index $J$ (Eq. 4), which is a network-wide topological metric computed strictly over the APs' raw client counts $n_i$ rather than flow-level QoS or capacity-normalized load ($n_i / C_i$). Whenever the heuristic proposes steering clients from an overloaded AP (utilization $\ge 0.8$) to a less loaded AP (utilization $< 0.6$), $J$ changes deterministically based on station counts alone; whenever $\delta_J > 5\%$, the second clause of Eq. (6) is automatically satisfied and bypasses all flow-level QoS checks ($T, L_{95}, \bar{\ell}$).
   - **Suggested Improvement:** Explicitly clarify in Section IV-B whether $\mathbf{k}(s_t)$ in Eq. (6) denotes the live telemetry KPI vector or the simulated baseline $f(s_t, \emptyset)$, provide the exact mathematical formula for $\delta_k$ (including zero-denominator handling), and discuss in Section IV how the $15.8\text{ ms}$ ceiling of Eq. (3) and the inclusion of network-wide client-count fairness $J$ in the disjunctive clause of Eq. (6) interact with the verifier's safety filtering (or consider separating topological count metrics from flow-level QoS metrics and scaling $s$ inversely with $C_i$).

2. **[I2.2] Incompleteness of the Max-Min Fair Sharing Equation and Unspecified Demand Reconstruction for Video and Web Flows (Section III-B and Section IV-A, Eqs. 2–3):**
   - **Potential Concern:** There are three mathematical and reproducibility gaps in the sharing and queueing formulation of Section IV-A:
     1. *Unsaturated Regime in Eq. (2):* In Eq. (2), the max-min fair share allocation $x_f = \min(\lambda_f, \theta_q)$ for queue $q$ is defined by solving the equality $\sum_{f \in q} \min(\lambda_f, \theta_q) = R_q$, where $R_q = \max(0, C_i - \text{the rates served to the higher queues})$. Mathematically, because $\min(\lambda_f, \theta_q) \le \lambda_f$ for all $\theta_q \in \mathbb{R}$, this equality has a real solution for $\theta_q$ only when the queue is saturated or overloaded ($\sum_{f \in q} \lambda_f \ge R_q$). When total demand in queue $q$ is strictly less than the available residual capacity ($\sum_{f \in q} \lambda_f < R_q$), $\sum_{f \in q} \min(\lambda_f, \theta_q) \le \sum_{f \in q} \lambda_f < R_q$, meaning no real value of $\theta_q$ satisfies Eq. (2).
     2. *Reconstructing Unthrottled Offered Demand $\lambda_f$ from Telemetry:* Both Eq. (2) and the cumulative load $\rho_q = \sum_{q' \preceq q} \sum_{f \in q'} \lambda_f / C_i$ in Eq. (3) require each flow's unthrottled offered demand $\lambda_f$ ("its source rate, cut to its rate limit"). However, Section III-B states that the twin rebuilds its state $s$ solely from the last 10 s of telemetry, which records each flow's `(measured throughput, latency, loss, queue, rate limit)`, while Section IV-A specifies a fixed median offered demand of $0.394\text{ Mbit/s}$ only for elastic bulk TCP flows. During congestion, an inelastic video flow's measured downlink throughput $x_f$ is throttled below its source demand $\lambda_f$ by packet drops, and the text does not specify how the twin reconstructs the unthrottled demand $\lambda_f$ from telemetry (e.g., via $\lambda_f = x_f / (1 - \ell_f)$, upstream switch counters, or static scenario metadata).
     3. *Web Traffic Participation and Priority Queue Mapping in Eqs. (2) and (3):* Section IV-A states that a web fetch "reports the rate of one page fetch, 0.8 of the capacity that the other traffic in its own or a higher queue leaves free," without specifying the default mapping of the three traffic classes (video, web, bulk TCP) to the three strict-priority queues (`priority`, `best effort`, `background`), whether web flows are excluded from the summation in Eq. (2) and evaluated post-hoc after video and bulk TCP flows within the same queue (and how multiple concurrent web flows divide that residual share), or what demand value $\lambda_f$ a web flow contributes to $\rho_q$ in Eq. (3).
   - **Suggested Improvement:** Reformulate Eq. (2) to handle the unsaturated regime explicitly (e.g., setting $x_f = \lambda_f$ when $\sum_{f \in q} \lambda_f \le R_q$, or writing $\sum_{f \in q} \min(\lambda_f, \theta_q) = \min\bigl(R_q, \sum_{f \in q} \lambda_f\bigr)$), state how $\lambda_f$ is derived from the telemetry state $s$ for video flows during congestion, and specify the queue mapping and exact expressions showing how web flows enter $R_q$ in Eq. (2) and $\rho_q$ in Eq. (3).

3. **[I2.3] Contradiction Between Single-Watch Isolation, Multi-Action Tick Execution, and Unverified State Assumptions During Rollback (Section IV-B and Section V / Algorithm 1):**
   - **Potential Concern:** Comparing the prose specification in Sections IV-B and V with Algorithm 1 (page 4) reveals two operational inconsistencies in how proposals and rollbacks are executed:
     1. *Multi-Action Execution vs. Single-Watch Isolation:* Section V states that *"While a change is being watched nothing new is proposed, so effects are not mixed up,"* and notes that *"The heuristics' proposals are verified and applied one at a time, each against the state of the tick and each with its own watch (they usually propose one, so interactions between proposals of one tick are not simulated)."* However, in Algorithm 1, the active-watch guard (`if an earlier change is still being watched then return`, lines 2–4) is checked only once *before* the proposal loop (`for each proposal p in P do`, lines 7–14), while lines 8–10 allow up to 3 proposals from the same tick to be verified sequentially against the same unmodified initial state $s$ (`VERIFY(s, p)`) and applied within the same tick with concurrent 30 s watches. If `PROPOSE(s)` yields more than one proposal in a tick (e.g., when two APs exceed $0.8$ utilization), verifying the second and third proposals against the un-updated state $s$ ignores state transitions induced by the first applied action (such as steering onto the same target AP), and running concurrent watches mixes their live KPI effects and makes rollback attribution in line 1 ambiguous. Furthermore, for proposals queued for operator approval (lines 11–12), Algorithm 1 specifies no state-staleness check or re-verification step if an operator approves the queued action in a later tick after $s$ has changed.
     2. *Absence of State-Bound Verification (S1) During Rollback:* In Section IV-B (S1), forward actions $a_t$ must satisfy hard safety and feasibility bounds $a_t \in \mathcal{A}(s_t)$ (requiring that target APs be up and provide a predicted signal $\ge -75\text{ dBm}$). However, in condition (S4) and Algorithm 1 (line 1), when a 30 s watch expires and detects a KPI regression $>10\%$ above the noise floor, the executor unconditionally rolls back the action at the start of the tick—before rebuilding the twin state from telemetry (line 5) and without checking whether the inverse action $a_t^{-1}$ still satisfies $\mathcal{A}(s_{t+30\text{s}})$ (specifically, whether the original AP is still operational).
   - **Suggested Improvement:** Clarify in Section V and Algorithm 1 whether at most one heuristic action can be applied per tick (terminating the `for` loop after the first `APPLY(p)` on line 10) or how sequential state updates ($s \leftarrow \text{simulate}(s, p)$) and concurrent watches are isolated when up to 3 actions are applied in one tick. Additionally, explicitly note in Section IV-B (S4) and Algorithm 1 (line 1) that executing an unconditional rollback without re-verifying (S1) against current telemetry assumes topological invariance over the 30 s watch window.

4. **[I2.4] Load-Independent Co-Channel Interference and Circular Testbed Rate-Cap Enforcement (Section IV-A, Eq. 1):**
   - **Potential Concern:** In Eq. (1) (page 2), the downlink capacity $C_i$ of AP $i$ is reduced by every administratively up co-channel AP $j \ne i$ within $60\text{ m}$ solely as a function of Euclidean distance $d_{ij}$, independent of the interfering AP's traffic load $\rho_j$ or airtime utilization $\mathcal{U}_j$, and the Mininet-WiFi testbed enforces this exact equation as a Linux traffic-control (`tc`) downlink rate cap. While Section IV-A candidly notes that this validates the sharing implementation rather than physical CSMA/CA contention and treats an idle co-channel AP like a busy one, this design has a direct structural consequence for the control problem in Section IV-B and Section V: because Eq. (1) is independent of the interfering AP's traffic load, steering clients away from an interfering co-channel AP (even until it carries zero traffic) or rate-limiting its flows yields *zero* predicted or emulated capacity relief $C_i$ to co-channel neighbors in both the twin and the testbed. Consequently, the formulation structurally renders co-channel capacity $C_i$ invariant to client steering and rate limiting, making a high-impact channel change (or taking an AP down) the only action capable of restoring $C_i$.
   - **Suggested Improvement:** Explicitly note in Section IV-A or Section V how enforcing Eq. (1) as a load-independent rate cap affects the action space of the control loop (specifically rendering co-channel capacity $C_i$ invariant to client steering and rate limiting), and consider noting how weighting $w(d_{ij})$ by the interferer's active utilization $\mathcal{U}_j$ (or normalized offered load $\min(1, \rho_j)$) in both the testbed emulation model and the twin would capture load-coupled airtime contention.

5. **[I2.5] Underspecified Action Scope in (S2), Medium-Impact Approval Gating, Heuristic Selection Rules, and Deterministic LLM Policy Compiler (Section III-B, Section IV-B, and Section V):**
   - **Potential Concern:** Several algorithmic and operational details required to independently reimplement the verifier, heuristic proposer, and LLM intent pipeline are omitted or described inconsistently across Sections III–V:
     1. *Scope of "Flows the Action Affects" in (S2) and (S4):* Section IV-B states that $\delta_k$ is computed "over the flows the action affects" and (S4) watches "the live KPIs of the flows the verdict compared," while Section VI (page 4) notes that "this evaluation cohort is not the set of flows a verdict compares, which is the action's own scope." However, Section IV-B never formally defines the action scope for each action type—for instance, when steering a subset of clients from $\text{AP}_{\text{src}}$ to $\text{AP}_{\text{dst}}$, whether the compared flow set includes only the steered stations' flows, all flows on $\text{AP}_{\text{src}}$, or all flows on both $\text{AP}_{\text{src}}$ and $\text{AP}_{\text{dst}}$ (whose existing flows experience increased load).
     2. *Inconsistency in Medium-Impact Action Gating:* Section III-B (page 2) states that among medium-impact actions, client steering is applied automatically when the twin accepts it, whereas rate-limit and transmit-power actions "wait for an operator until their predictions are validated." However, Constraint (S3) in Section IV-B and lines 9–12 of Algorithm 1 state only that *high-impact* actions wait for operator approval, leaving ambiguous how unvalidated medium-impact actions are gated in Algorithm 1.
     3. *Heuristic Granularity and Selection Criteria:* Section V states that an overloaded AP ($\ge 0.8$ utilization) "sheds up to 30% of its clients to APs below 0.6, strongest predicted signal first," and that a congested co-channel AP "moves to the least loaded one." It is not specified whether a single heuristic proposal $p \in P$ steers one client or a batch of up to $\lfloor 0.3 n_i \rfloor$ clients, which stations on the overloaded AP are selected first to be steered, or what exact metric defines the "least loaded" channel (e.g., number of co-channel APs, sum of utilizations $\mathcal{U}$, or distance-weighted capacity reduction $w(d_{ij})$ in Eq. 1).
     4. *Deterministic LLM Policy Compiler and Policy Constraints:* Section V states that the LLM parses operator text into a `Policy` JSON schema (`scope`, `objectives`, `hard constraints`) and "a deterministic compiler turns the policy into bounded actions," while Section IV-B mentions that "the verifier also checks any operator policy's constraints and objectives, omitted here." Neither the deterministic compiler's mapping rules from declarative `Policy` fields to the seven concrete action primitives nor the verifier's policy-constraint rules are summarized.
   - **Suggested Improvement:** Formally define the flow cohort compared by the verifier for each action type in Section IV-B (particularly whether target-AP flows are included during steering verification), align Constraint (S3) and Algorithm 1 with the medium-impact gating rule in Section III-B, specify the station-selection and channel-ranking rules in Section V, and concisely summarize the translation and verification rules for LLM-compiled policies.
