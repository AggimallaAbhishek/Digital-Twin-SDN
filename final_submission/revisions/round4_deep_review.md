# HIGH LEVEL SUMMARY
---
## Summary
This paper presents a safety-gated closed control loop for software-defined campus Wi-Fi networks that verifies candidate actions—proposed either by a heuristic load-balancing optimizer or compiled deterministically from schema-validated Large Language Model (LLM) natural-language policies—inside an analytical digital twin before execution. The digital twin models per-access-point (AP) fluid airtime sharing, distance-weighted co-channel interference, and calibrated M/M/1/K queueing latency in sub-millisecond time, while an executor monitors post-action live telemetry over a 30-second watch window to automatically roll back regressions. Evaluated across 27 emulated Mininet-WiFi runs spanning lecture flash crowd, AP failure, and co-channel interference scenarios, the study transparently reports valuable negative results: steady-state analytical accuracy (4.0% error) fails to carry over to closed-loop re-association transients (where predictions perform no better than a "nothing changes" baseline and reject only 2 of 23 harmful actions), and blind watch-and-rollback mechanisms can degrade performance below plain SDN when concurrent infrastructure failures occur.

## Strengths
* The paper demonstrates commendable scientific transparency and rigor by thoroughly reporting and diagnosing negative empirical results, offering critical cautionary insights into how steady-state twin accuracy fails during closed-loop transients and how automated rollbacks can backfire during concurrent AP failures.
* The end-to-end safety-gated architecture cleanly adapts autonomic monitoring, pre-deployment performance verification, and schema-constrained LLM policy compilation to shared-medium enterprise Wi-Fi, ensuring via deterministic compilation and execution bounds that no syntactically invalid LLM output reaches the network.
* The experimental artifacts and evaluation pipeline are highly reproducible, providing an automated virtual wireless SDN testbed, seeded disruption scenarios, and scripts that regenerate all tables and figures from the recorded execution ledgers.

## Weaknesses
* **[W1]** The experimental comparison between controller variants V2 (no twin) and V3 (full system) is confounded because V3 simultaneously introduces both the digital twin verifier and a human operator approval gate for high-impact actions while running the evaluation completely unattended; please consider adding an auto-approving V3 or operator-gated V2 ablation, as well as a baseline with standard client-side roaming, on a fresh set of random seeds disjoint from the 3 calibration seeds (Reference Paper: IEEE Standard for Information Technology—Telecommunications and Information Exchange between Systems - Local and Metropolitan Area Networks—Specific Requirements - Part 11: Wireless LAN Medium Access Control (MAC) and Physical Layer (PHY) Specifications, IEEE LAN/MAN Standards Committee, 2021).
* **[W2]** The analytical digital twin relies on a load-independent distance-based capacity formula that is circularly enforced as a rate cap on the testbed itself and a calibrated M/M/1/K latency surrogate hard-capped at 15.8 ms (well below the 50 ms recovery threshold), which fails to capture physical wireless contention or re-association transients; the authors might consider incorporating load-dependent contention models or learned queueing surrogates to improve closed-loop predictive fidelity (Reference Paper: Performance analysis of the IEEE 802.11 distributed coordination function, Giuseppe Bianchi, 2000; Reference Paper: Performance anomaly of 802.11b, Martin Heusse et al., 2003; Reference Paper: RouteNet-Fermi: Network modeling with graph neural networks, Miquel Ferriol-Galmés et al., 2023; Reference Paper: From Simulators to Digital Twins for Enabling Emerging Cellular Networks: A Tutorial and Survey, Ali Imran et al., 2025).
* **[W3]** The safety verification and watch-and-rollback rules exhibit structural vulnerabilities under concurrent network disruptions: the disjunctive acceptance criterion in Eq. (6) allows trivial improvements in client-count fairness ($J$) to bypass severe flow-level regressions, while the executor blindly rolls actions back onto failed APs without re-verifying topological liveness or hard bounds (S1).
* **[W4]** Although the neuro-symbolic LLM intent pipeline builds effectively on prior verified configuration synthesis frameworks, it is evaluated strictly offline outside the closed control loop on a small, custom set of 30 unambiguous intents without testing whether the digital twin catches the local model's 9 semantic translation errors or benchmarking against broader, adversarial intent suites (Reference Paper: NetConfEval: Can LLMs facilitate network configuration?, Changjie Wang et al., 2024; Reference Paper: What do LLMs need to synthesize correct router configurations?, Rajdeep Mondal et al., 2023; Reference Paper: Intent-based management of next-generation networks: an LLM-centric approach, Abdelkader Mekrache et al., 2024; Reference Paper: Net2d-LLM: Translating Structured Network Intents into CLI using LLMs with Execution in a Network Digital Twin, Jerônimo Menezes et al., 2025).

## Potential Issues And Suggestions
### [Introduction, Hypotheses, and Related Work] (Pages: 1-2)
#### Potential Mistakes and Improvements

1. **[I1.1] Title and Abstract Framing of the LLM Pipeline vs. the Closed-Loop Evaluation Scope (Title, Abstract, and Section I, Page 1)**
   - *Observation:* The Title (*"A Twin-Gated Control Loop for Campus Wi-Fi: Verifying SDN and LLM-Proposed Actions in a Digital Twin Before They Reach the Network"*), the second sentence of the Abstract (*"We present a control loop in which no action, whether proposed by a heuristic optimizer or by a large language model (LLM) that translates an operator's intent, reaches the network without an accepted verdict from a digital twin"*), and Section I (*"We ask whether this makes an AI-driven controller both more effective and safer than acting directly, and whether an LLM can drive it from natural language"*) frame the contribution as a unified closed control loop verified on the network. While Section I subsequently states that *"The closed loop of the campaign runs a heuristic optimizer; the LLM pipeline is evaluated separately (H3),"* the architectural details in Section V (Algorithm 1 caption: *"Actions compiled from an LLM policy are verified jointly through the API and applied only by an operator's call, not by the loop"*) and Section VII-C (*"On a 30-intent test set the cloud model translated 30 of 30 correctly... all 6 of 6 LLM-proposed actions have their twin verdict in the audit log (the explainer only suggests them, and none was applied)"*) reveal that LLM-compiled actions are never executed by the 5 s control loop and none were ever applied to the emulated network during the evaluation.
   - *Potential Concern:* A reader of the Title, Abstract, and Contributions (iv)–(v) may reasonably infer that the 30 translated LLM intents were compiled into actions, verified by the digital twin, and applied to the testbed, or that the LLM drove the active control loop in a subset of runs. Revising the Abstract and Section I to explicitly state that the 30-intent benchmark evaluates open-loop translation and compilation accuracy (syntax, schema validity, and semantic fidelity to the policy representation) offline—and that only the heuristic optimizer was executed on the live emulated network—would prevent misinterpretation of the experimental scope.

2. **[I1.2] Unjustified Baseline Thresholds and Censoring Horizon Dependency in Hypotheses H1 and H3 (Section I and Section II, Pages 1–2)**
   - *Observation:* Section I introduces quantitative pass/fail thresholds for Hypothesis **H1** (at least a $30\%$ reduction in capped mean recovery time over plain SDN) and Hypothesis **H3** (at least $20$ of $30$ intents translated correctly, i.e., $66.7\%$ accuracy) without providing theoretical or empirical justification for these specific cutoff values.
   - *Potential Concern:*
     - For **H1**, as explained in Section II (*Programmable WLANs*), stations in the plain SDN baseline (V1) never roam away from overloaded or interfered APs on their own, guaranteeing by design that V1 never recovers within the 600 s window in the congestion and interference scenarios ($T_{\text{rec}}$ is deterministically right-censored at $T_{\text{end}} - t_d$, i.e., 390 s and 420 s). Because any fixed percentage reduction relative to a deterministic right-censoring cap depends directly on the chosen simulation duration $T_{\text{end}} = 600\text{ s}$ rather than on an active baseline recovery process, Section I should briefly explain the rationale for the $30\%$ threshold relative to the 600 s horizon and clarify why V1 without native IEEE 802.11k/v/r client roaming was selected as the primary baseline.
     - For **H3**, a threshold of $20/30$ ($66.7\%$) on a custom internal set of 30 unambiguous intents (where 17 of the 30 intents involve only a single objective in a single traffic class, and the primary cloud model achieves $30/30$ with zero repair attempts) is relatively permissive and exhibits a ceiling effect. It also does not directly probe the failure modes emphasized in Section I's motivation (*"LLM output can be wrong, incomplete or unsafe"*), such as ambiguous, conflicting, or adversarial intents. Briefly motivating the $20/30$ threshold in Section I and characterizing how the custom 30-intent wireless set relates in difficulty and structure to established configuration benchmarks cited in Section II (such as NetConfEval [9]) would strengthen the hypothesis framing.

3. **[I1.3] Contextualizing the Analytical Twin Against Classical 802.11 Contention Models and Verified-LLM Literature (Section I and Section II, Pages 1–2)**
   - *Observation:* In Section II (*Learned performance models*), the paper cites Bianchi's Markov chain model for saturated CSMA/CA [5] and Heusse et al.'s analysis of the 802.11 performance anomaly [6], noting that *"Our model needs only per-AP capacity and distance, but it ignores CSMA/CA backoff and the anomaly, a simplification we state in Section VIII."* Furthermore, Section I (Contribution (i)) notes that the testbed enforces an explicit model of co-channel interference that the emulator does not produce itself, which Section IV-A reveals is the exact same load-independent distance-based rate cap (Eq. 1) used by the analytical twin.
   - *Potential Concern:* Given the paper's central negative finding that the twin's closed-loop predictions have median relative errors of $56.7\%$–$70.5\%$ and reject only 2 of 23 harmful actions (failing **H2**), Sections I and II do not explain why a load-independent static capacity cap and a capped M/M/1/K latency surrogate were chosen over classical load-dependent contention models ([5], [6]) or learned queueing surrogates ([3], [4]). Clarifying in Section I or II why both the testbed and the twin adopt an identical, load-independent capacity equation, and explicitly framing the twin evaluation as a test of whether a minimalist steady-state analytical model suffices for 5 s closed-loop control during re-association transients, would better connect the literature review to the negative results previewed in the Abstract. Additionally, in the *LLM-based intent networking* paragraph of Section II, briefly acknowledging prior work that pairs LLM configuration synthesis with formal verifiers in a feedback loop (beyond standalone wired verifiers like Batfish [10] and VeriFlow [11]) would help contextualize the paper's deterministic policy compiler and verifier architecture.

### [System Model, Analytical Digital Twin Formulation, and Control Loop Methodology] (Pages: 2-4)
#### Potential Mistakes and Improvements

*Verification Summary:* The mathematical derivations in Section IV-A (Eqs. 1–4) and the control problem formulations in Section IV-B (Eqs. 5–6) are internally consistent:

- **Capacity Model (Eq. 1):** With $C = 4.6\text{ Mbit/s}$, $d_1 = 30\text{ m}$, $d_2 = 60\text{ m}$, and distance $d_{14} = 50\text{ m}$ between $\text{ap1}$ and $\text{ap4}$, the interference weight is $w(50) = (60 - 50)/(60 - 30) = 1/3$, yielding $C_1 = C_4 = 4.6 / (1 + 1/3) = 3.45\text{ Mbit/s}$, matching Section III-A.

- **Strict-Priority Max-Min Fair Sharing (Eq. 2):** The residual capacity $R_q = \max\big(0, C_i - \sum_{q' \prec q} \sum_{f \in q'} x_f\big)$ and intra-queue water-filling formulation $\sum_{f \in q} \min(\lambda_f, \theta_q) = \min\big(R_q, \sum_{f \in q} \lambda_f\big)$ are algebraically sound.

- **Queueing Surrogate (Eq. 3):** The expected occupancy $L(\rho, K) = \frac{\rho}{1-\rho} - \frac{(K+1)\rho^{K+1}}{1-\rho^{K+1}}$ matches the exact finite-buffer M/M/1/K mean system size formula with continuous limit $\lim_{\rho \to 1} L(\rho, K) = K/2$, and for $d_0 = 0.8\text{ ms}$, $\sigma = 2.5\text{ ms}$, and $K = 6$, the asymptotic delay ceiling as $\rho_q \to \infty$ is $d_0 + \sigma K = 15.8\text{ ms}$.

- **Time to Recover (Eq. 5):** The right-censoring intervals $T_{\text{end}} - t_d$ for $T_{\text{end}} = 600\text{ s}$ and $t_d \in \{210, 240, 180\}\text{ s}$ evaluate to $390\text{ s}, 360\text{ s},$ and $420\text{ s}$, matching the reported bounds.

Although the paper candidly highlights several limitations of these formulations as negative results in later sections, the following substantive methodological and reproducibility issues within Sections III–V warrant attention:

1. **[I2.1] Methodological Circularity and Load-Independence in the Co-Channel Interference Model (Section III-A & Section IV-A, Eq. 1)**
   - **Potential Concern:** Because the underlying Mininet-WiFi setup does not natively generate physical-layer co-channel contention, the testbed artificially enforces the exact analytical formula of Eq. (1) as a static downlink traffic-control (`tc`) rate cap on each AP's queue whenever a same-channel AP is administratively up. While Section IV-A transparently notes this design choice, it introduces two structural limitations for evaluating a digital twin and control loop:
     1. *Validation Circularity:* Validating the analytical twin's steady-state capacity sharing against a testbed that explicitly enforces the twin's own closed-form equation verifies software implementation consistency rather than testing the analytical model's fidelity against independent wireless contention physics (such as IEEE 802.11 CSMA/CA backoff dynamics or mixed-rate airtime anomalies).
     2. *Insensitivity to Interferer Traffic Load:* In Eq. (1), $C_i$ depends solely on inter-AP distance $d_{ij}$, channel assignment, and administrative `up`/`down` status, completely ignoring the actual traffic demand $\lambda_f$ or airtime utilization $\mathcal{U}_j$ of the interfering AP $j$. Consequently, an idle co-channel AP transmitting only beacons penalizes neighboring APs identically to a saturated one. By mathematical construction, low- and medium-impact actions such as steering clients away from an interfering AP or rate-limiting its flows yield zero capacity relief to neighboring co-channel APs in both the twin and the testbed, artificially restricting effective remediation in the co-channel interference scenario exclusively to high-impact actions (channel changes or taking an AP down).
   - **Suggested Improvement:** The physical realism of both the testbed rate-limiting script and the analytical twin can be strengthened by weighting the interference factor $w(d_{ij})$ in Eq. (1) by the interfering AP's active airtime utilization $\mathcal{U}_j \in [0, 1]$ (or a load-dependent CSMA/CA airtime approximation), or by enabling an independent wireless medium simulator such as `wmediumd` in Mininet-WiFi so that testbed contention is not governed by the twin's own static equation.

2. **[I2.2] Mismatch Between the M/M/1/K Latency Surrogate Ceiling, Priority-Queue Dynamics, and Closed-Loop Transients (Section IV-A, Eqs. 2–3 & Section IV-B, Eqs. 5–6)**
   - **Potential Concern:** As a predictive model for the safe control problem in Section IV-B, the latency and equilibrium formulation in Section IV-A exhibits four structural disconnects:
     1. *Inability to Predict Recovery Threshold Violations or Saturation Deltas:* With calibrated parameters $d_0 = 0.8\text{ ms}$, $\sigma = 2.5\text{ ms}$, and buffer limit $K = 6$, the maximum latency Eq. (3) can ever output as $\rho_q \to \infty$ is strictly upper-bounded by $d_0 + \sigma K = 15.8\text{ ms}$. Because the safe control recovery criterion in Eq. (5) requires 95th-percentile latency $L_{95}(\tau) \le 50\text{ ms}$, the twin is mathematically incapable of predicting a violation of the $50\text{ ms}$ SLA threshold. Furthermore, once both the baseline state $s_t$ and a candidate post-action state are heavily overloaded ($\rho_q \gg 1$), both predictions plateau near $15.8\text{ ms}$, causing the predicted relative latency change $\delta_{L_{95}}$ in Eq. (6) to approach $0\%$ regardless of how much congestion is added or relieved.
     2. *Priority-Queue Starvation Omission:* Under strict priority scheduling (Eq. 2), lower-priority queues see their available capacity reduced to $R_q = \max\big(0, C_i - \sum_{q' \prec q} \sum_{f \in q'} x_f\big)$ and experience preemption delay that scales inversely with $(1 - \rho_{\prec q})(1 - \rho_{\preceq q})$. In contrast, Eq. (3) plugs the cumulative unthrottled demand ratio $\rho_q = \sum_{q' \preceq q} \sum_{f \in q'} \lambda_f / C_i$ into a single-class M/M/1/K formula, which does not capture lower-priority queue starvation as higher-priority demand approaches $C_i$.
     3. *Station-Level Latency Aggregation Across Priority Queues:* Section IV-A states that *"all flows of a station report the latency of its highest-priority queue, this is a percentile over stations, not over packet delays."* Because a higher-priority queue $q_1 \prec q_2$ has $\rho_{q_1} \le \rho_{q_2}$ and therefore $d_{q_1} \le d_{q_2}$, assigning a station's highest-priority queue delay to all of its flows understates the queueing delay experienced by best-effort or background flows whenever that station concurrently runs a higher-priority flow. Moreover, taking the nearest-rank 95th percentile of *mean* station delays across at most 20 stations does not capture the 95th percentile of the packet-level waiting-time distribution.
     4. *Steady-State Equilibrium vs. Re-Association Transients:* Eqs. (1)–(3) model memoryless steady-state equilibrium and omit the multi-second IEEE 802.11 re-association and transport-layer transient triggered by explicit client steering, even though the verifier's predictions are evaluated against the `[10 s, 30 s]` live watch window (S4) where transient effects remain active.
   - **Suggested Improvement:** Consider replacing $\rho_q$ in Eq. (3) with queue $q$'s demand relative to its residual capacity $R_q$ (or a standard non-preemptive priority-queue delay expression), recalibrating $\sigma$ and $K$ (or adding an explicit overload penalty when $\rho_q \ge 1$) so the surrogate spans the $>50\text{ ms}$ regime, attributing the predicted delay $d_q$ of each flow's actual queue $q$ rather than the station's highest-priority queue, and incorporating an empirical re-association transient penalty calibrated from the training split's scripted steering runs.

3. **[I2.3] Permissive Disjunctive Verifier Rule (`OR`), Zero-Baseline Discontinuities, and Unmodeled Allow-Listed Actions (Section III-B, Section IV-A, & Section IV-B, Eq. 6)**
   - **Potential Concern:** The verifier acceptance rule in Eq. (6) over the KPI vector $\mathbf{k} = (T, L_{95}, \bar{\ell}, J)$,
     $$\text{accept} \iff \big(\forall k : \delta_k \ge -5\big) \lor \big(\exists k : \delta_k > 5\big),$$
     connects a global non-regression guard and a single-KPI improvement clause via a logical disjunction ($\lor$). Although the text notes that Eq. (6) is deliberately permissive, the formulation and action-set mapping exhibit three structural vulnerabilities as a safety gate:
     1. *Unbounded Regression Bypass via Client-Count Fairness $J$:* Because the two clauses are joined by a logical OR ($\lor$), the second clause ($\exists k : \delta_k > 5$) has no lower bound on the remaining metrics: a $>5\%$ predicted improvement in any single KPI forces the verifier to accept an action even if the twin simultaneously predicts catastrophic regressions ($\delta_k \ll -5\%$, or $\delta_{\bar{\ell}} = -\infty$) in throughput, latency, or packet loss. Crucially, because Jain's fairness index $J$ in Eq. (4) is evaluated network-wide solely over AP client counts $n_i$ (ignoring flow demands $\lambda_f$ and heterogeneous AP capacities $C_i$, whereas $T, L_{95}, \bar{\ell}$ are evaluated locally over the action's flow scope), any client-steering action that equalizes station counts across APs by $>5\%$ satisfies $\delta_J > 5$ and automatically bypasses all flow-level performance checks.
     2. *Zero-Baseline Edge-Case Discontinuities ($\delta_k = \pm\infty$):* Immediately below Eq. (6), $\delta_k$ is defined as a relative change $100(k - \hat{k})/|k|$ for latency and loss, with $\delta_k = +\infty$ or $-\infty$ whenever $k = 0 \ne \hat{k}$ represents an improvement or degradation. Under this definition, an infinitesimal packet loss appearing where baseline loss was $k = 0$ yields $\delta_{\bar{\ell}} = -\infty$ (violating the first clause even for negligible loss), whereas restoring any infinitesimal positive throughput from $k = 0$ yields $\delta_T = +\infty > 5$ (unconditionally satisfying the second clause regardless of target-AP congestion).
     3. *Trivial Auto-Acceptance of Unmodeled `transmit power` Actions:* Section III-B and constraint (S1) define `transmit power` (steps up to $3\text{ dB}$) as an allow-listed medium-impact action, yet Section IV-A states that Eqs. (1)–(4) and the path-loss formula used by (S1) do not depend on transmit power at all. Consequently, the twin always predicts $\hat{\mathbf{k}} = \mathbf{k}$ ($\delta_k = 0$ for all $k$) and vacuously accepts any transmit-power step via the first clause ($\forall k : 0 \ge -5$). While the heuristics do not propose transmit-power changes, an LLM-compiled policy can propose any allow-listed action.
   - **Suggested Improvement:** Formulate the safety gate (S2) conjunctively so that bounded non-regression across flow-level performance KPIs is a mandatory safety floor—e.g., $(\forall k : \delta_k \ge -\tau_{\text{reg}}) \land (\exists k : \delta_k > \tau_{\text{imp}})$, or prevent topological client-count fairness $J$ from overriding throughput, latency, and loss regressions—and use an absolute percentage-point change (or a small positive floor $\epsilon_k$ in the denominator) for loss $\bar{\ell}$ when $k = 0$. In addition, either couple `transmit power` to the path-loss signal equation and interference weights $w(d_{ij})$ or reject action types whose physical effects are unmodeled in Eqs. (1)–(4).

4. **[I2.4] Vulnerabilities in Watch-and-Rollback Enforcement (S4), Control-Loop Freezing, and Multi-Action Ticks (Section IV-B & Section V, Algorithm 1)**
   - **Potential Concern:** The operational mechanics in Section IV-B (constraints S3–S4) and Section V (Algorithm 1) contain three structural failure modes:
     1. *Blind Rollback Without Re-Verifying (S1) or AP Liveness (S4 & Algorithm 1, Line 1):* When the $30\text{ s}$ watch measures a $>10\%$ KPI regression above the noise floor, Line 1 of Algorithm 1 rolls the action back *"without checking (S1) again, so the rollback assumes the topology did not change during the watch."* Because the watch monitors live telemetry over $30\text{ s}$ without filtering out concurrent infrastructure faults, an exogenous AP failure occurring during the $30\text{ s}$ watch window of an earlier client steer causes a KPI drop that the watch misattributes to the steer, blindly rolling the station back onto the dead AP and leaving it disconnected for the rest of the run.
     2. *Control Loop Paralysis During Watch Windows and Stale Operator Approvals (Algorithm 1, Lines 2–4 & S3):* While any applied change is being watched ($30\text{ s}$, or 6 ticks of the $5\text{ s}$ loop), Algorithm 1 (Lines 2–4) returns early and proposes nothing new, blinding the controller for up to $30\text{ s}$ if an external fault occurs shortly after a routine action. Similarly, under constraint (S3), actions queued for operator approval (Line 12) are later applied *"as verified, not re-verified against a later state,"* ignoring any topology or load changes that occurred while waiting in the queue.
     3. *Sequential Single-Action Verification vs. Confounded Multi-Action Watches (Algorithm 1, Lines 7–14):* When the heuristics propose up to 3 actions in a single $5\text{ s}$ tick, Line 8 verifies each proposal $p \in P$ individually against the *same* initial pre-tick state $s$ ($\hat{\mathbf{k}} = f(s, p)$) rather than sequentially updating the simulated state ($s \leftarrow \text{apply}(s, p)$) or verifying the accepted set jointly (unlike LLM-compiled policies, which are verified jointly). Moreover, actions applied in the same tick run overlapping $30\text{ s}$ watches over nearly identical telemetry windows, preventing Line 1 from attributing a measured regression to a specific offending action.
   - **Suggested Improvement:** In Algorithm 1 and Section IV-B: (i) require Line 1 to check hard bounds (S1) and verify that the rollback target AP is currently `up` in the latest telemetry state before executing a rollback, and immediately abort/release active watches upon verifiable infrastructure-down events; (ii) update the simulated state $s$ sequentially across accepted proposals within a single tick in Line 8 (or verify tick proposals jointly); and (iii) re-run `VERIFY(s_now, p)` before executing any delayed operator-approved action.

5. **[I2.5] Incomplete Specification of Mathematical Definitions, Flow Scopes, and Model Parameters for Independent Reproducibility (Section III, Section IV, & Section V)**
   - **Potential Concern:** While external configuration files are provided in the companion package, several mathematical definitions and algorithmic parameters required to independently understand and reimplement the analytical twin $f(s, a)$, verifier, and heuristics directly from the text of Sections III–V are omitted or ambiguous:
     1. *Measured AP Utilization $\mathcal{U}(t)$ vs. Twin Queue Load $\rho_q$ (Section IV-A, Section IV-B, & Section V):* Section IV-B defines the utilization $\mathcal{U}(t)$ of an AP (used in Eq. 5 and the $\ge 0.8$ / $< 0.6$ heuristic triggers in Section V) as *"the traffic it serves (measured) divided by $C_i$,"* whereas Eq. (3) defines queue load $\rho_q = \sum_{q' \preceq q} \sum_{f \in q'} \lambda_f / C_i$ using offered source demand. Because measured served downlink traffic on a queue rate-capped at $C_i$ cannot exceed $C_i$ in steady state (dropped packets are not served), it is ambiguous whether $\mathcal{U}(t)$ measures offered queue ingress traffic prior to drops, includes uplink traffic, or how it behaves relative to the $0.8$ threshold during overload.
     2. *Configured Per-Class Demands $\lambda_f$, Web Flow Sharing, and Video Demand Inversion (Section IV-A):* Eq. (2) relies on configured source rates $\lambda_f$ for bulk TCP, web fetch demand, and unmeasured/dead video fallback, but only the bulk TCP median rate ($0.394\text{ Mbit/s}$) is stated in the text; the configured numerical demands for video and web flows are omitted. Furthermore, Section IV-A states that a web flow *"takes part in the sharing and in $\rho_q$ with its configured fetch demand, but the rate it reports is that of one page fetch, $0.8$ of the capacity that the other traffic in its own or a higher queue leaves free."* The exact algebraic formula for reported web throughput when multiple web or elastic flows compete within the same queue $q$ in Eq. (2) is not written out. Similarly, measured video demand is estimated as *"its received rate divided by $1 - \ell_f$,"* while simulated video loss is defined as *"$\ell_f = 1 - x_f / \lambda_f$ plus a base loss of $0.2\%$"*; clarifying whether the $0.002$ base loss offset is subtracted before inverting $1 - \ell_f$ avoids a slight systematic overestimation of $\lambda_f$ on each refresh.
     3. *Spatial Coordinates for Inter-AP Distances $d_{ij}$ and Path-Loss Prediction (Section III-A, Section III-B, & Section IV-A):* Both Eq. (1) and the log-distance path-loss model in Section IV-A ($-16\text{ dBm}$ at $1\text{ m}$, exponent $4.0$, $-75\text{ dBm}$ threshold in S1, corresponding to a maximum range of $10^{(75-16)/40} \approx 29.85\text{ m}$) require the 2D coordinates of the 4 APs and 20 stations, yet Section III-A specifies only $d_{14} = 50\text{ m}$, and Section III-B states that the twin state $s$ stores each station's `(AP, zone)` rather than exact $(x, y)$ coordinates.
     4. *Action-Scope Flow Set for Non-Steering Actions, Compiler Rules, and Hyperparameters (Section IV-B & Section V):* Section IV-B defines the verifier comparison scope in Eq. (6) for a client steer (source and target AP flows), but does not specify which APs' flows constitute the comparison scope for a *channel change* (which alters $C_j$ for all co-channel neighbors on both the old and new channels via Eq. 1). In addition, Section IV-B (S2) omits the verifier's operator-policy constraint checks (*"omitted here"*), and Section V omits the deterministic compiler's mapping rules from the JSON `Policy` schema to the seven allow-listed actions, the numerical values of the four 95th-percentile watch noise floors in (S4), and the hyperparameters for Holt's linear smoothing ($\alpha, \beta$, moving-average window) and the $30\text{ s}$ Isolation Forest (feature vector and contamination rate).
   - **Suggested Improvement:** Summarize the AP/station coordinates (or inter-AP distance matrix $d_{ij}$), configured per-class demands $\lambda_f$, per-KPI watch noise floors, and forecasting/detection hyperparameters in a compact parameter table in Section III or IV; provide the explicit algebraic expressions for $\mathcal{U}(t)$, reported web-flow throughput, and the channel-change flow scope in Eq. (6); and briefly state the deterministic policy compiler mapping rules in Section V.

   * *Potential Concern:* In Section I, the primary research question asks whether making a digital twin the gate that every change must pass *"makes an AI-driven controller both more effective and safer than acting directly,"* and defines Hypothesis **H1** around V3 recovering at least $30\%$ faster than V1 across all three scenarios.
